A Coin Is a Chain of Custody

There are no account balances in Bitcoin. A coin is an unspent transaction output (a UTXO). Spending consumes old outputs and creates new ones, each sealed to the next owner — so every coin carries an unbroken line back to where it was minted. Click any coin to trace it.

Tip: click a coin (the white/teal boxes) to light up its history.

How to read this

Black squares are transactions. The boxes they point to are outputs — chunks of coin assigned to an owner. An arrow into a transaction is an input: it spends an earlier output. So value flows left to right, output → transaction → new output.

Coinbase is the special transaction that mints new coin with no input — the origin of every chain of custody here. The teal-outlined boxes on the right are the current UTXO set: the only coins that can still be spent.

Spendable balances = sum of each owner's UTXOs

A wallet "balance" is just the total of the unspent outputs locked to your keys.

Ownership — why only you can spend your UTXOs

Each output is locked to an owner's public key (their address). To spend it you attach a signature made with the matching secret private key; anyone can verify the signature with the public key, but no one can forge it or work back to the secret.

This is where prime numbers actually live in Bitcoin — the signatures use elliptic-curve math over a prime field. Mining (the other plate) is brute-force hashing, not prime-finding.

Teaching model: amounts and owners are illustrative. Total coin is conserved across every transaction (inputs in = outputs out). Real Bitcoin works exactly this way — every payment is a graph of consumed and created outputs. — Plate 02 of the blockchain set.
